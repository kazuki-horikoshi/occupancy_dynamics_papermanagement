\noindent\textbf{ABSTRACT}
Occupant-centric HVAC control in shared offices must aggregate several personal comfort models into one room-level setpoint, yet hybrid and activity-based work change both the number and composition of occupants present.
This study quantifies when daily or real-time group comfort models justify their additional occupancy information.
Personal comfort models were developed for 39 participants from over 2,000 thermal-preference responses collected over four weeks in a Singapore office building, and an occupancy-log-based Monte Carlo simulation compared a static group model (regular room members), a daily model (daily attendees), a real-time model (occupants present at each interval), and a fixed 24$^\circ$C baseline.
Across four rooms, the real-time model achieved the highest mean comfort probability at every tested group size and, at the largest sizes,
exceeded the baseline by 12--15 and daily aggregation by 6--8 percentage
points; the static model approached the baseline as group size increased.
Real-time setpoint changes became smaller as mean occupancy increased but remained about 0.8$^\circ$C at the upper end of the observed daily mean-occupancy range, and the share of control intervals requiring an actionable setpoint step rose from about 37\% at low occupant turnover to roughly 90\% near complete turnover.
These findings demonstrate that group size, daily mean occupancy, and occupant turnover jointly shape the practical value of higher-resolution comfort aggregation, providing criteria for selecting group comfort models in shared offices.
